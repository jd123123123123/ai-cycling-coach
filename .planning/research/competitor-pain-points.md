# Competitor Pain Points: What Athletes Hate About Incumbent Apps

**Purpose.** This document synthesizes user complaints across Garmin Connect, Whoop, Strava, Oura, Apple Fitness, TrainingPeaks, and Intervals.icu, drawn from Reddit, App Store reviews, community forums, Trustpilot, DC Rainmaker comment threads, the5krunner, and official community hubs. Quotes are short and preserved close to verbatim where sourced; paraphrases are called out. The goal: an AI cycling coach dashboard + chat that learns from what users have been begging for (and what they hate) for years.

**Sources last sampled:** April 2026. Claims without a specific URL are consolidated from multi-thread reading and should be treated as widely-reported rather than single-source.

---

## Executive Summary: Top 10 Universal Pain Points (Ranked by Frequency)

1. **Data density with no hierarchy.** Every app drowns users in metrics but refuses to say "this is what matters today." Garmin's main screen is "an extremely confusing long scrolling list with complex icons." Whoop users: "scores feel like a black box." Intervals.icu: "quite overwhelming at first glance."
2. **Buried navigation.** Multi-level menus for basic info. Garmin: "more > health & fitness > steps > daily steps" to see daily steps. Users "hunt through multiple menus to find items they previously could display on a single page."
3. **Sync failures and data loss.** Apple Fitness: "cycling workouts stopped showing up as Live Activities." Oura: "workouts that vanish after syncing." Strava: "activities selectively not pushed to feed." These are trust-destroying.
4. **Paywall creep on features that used to be free.** Strava's Year in Sport ($80/yr), Garmin Connect+ subscription, Oura's "pointless without the subscription," Apple Fitness+ upsells inside workout summaries.
5. **Scores without "so what?".** Whoop recovery, Oura readiness, Garmin Body Battery — users can see the number but not what to do with it. "What confusion about what to do with recovery scores."
6. **Scores that contradict lived experience.** Oura: "the app often shows restful sleep when I barely got any." Whoop: "doesn't match how I feel." Destroys trust fast.
7. **Mobile/web feature parity gaps.** TrainingPeaks: Structured Workout Builder is desktop-only. Garmin: custom dashboards removed from web. Users live on mobile and resent "go to a laptop to do this."
8. **Social feed rot.** Strava feed sparse despite following 250+ people; "AI slop posts from new free accounts"; kudos limits (~20/day).
9. **Cycling-specific neglect in general apps.** Apple Watch credits only 21/65 cycling minutes because "coasting doesn't count." Apple Fitness VO2max excludes cycling entirely. Oura Activity Score under-weights volume.
10. **Updates that regress what worked.** Garmin Connect v5 redesign: "the vibrant color coding that signaled goal achievement is gone, replaced by subtle schemes many see as a step backward." Beta feedback "fell on deaf ears."

## Per-Product Sections

### Garmin Connect

Garmin Connect has the deepest data pool in consumer endurance and arguably the worst interface-to-data ratio. The 2024 "Connect v5" redesign united users in complaint.

**Navigation / IA.**
- "The main menu is extremely confusing with too much information condensed in a long scrolling list, represented with complex icons" (UX case study aggregating forum complaints).
- "Users have to hunt through multiple menus to find items they previously could display on a single page" (Garmin Forums, "Menu options missing left side").
- Training Status tile now uses "generic labels instead of exposing the raw training-load number."
- Custom dashboards on the web app were removed in the redesign.

**Scrolling / density.**
- "Stats cards are larger and require more scrolling to see the same information" (gadgetsandwearables.com, April 2024).
- Steps, sleep, stairs, HR are "scattered across several different tabs."

**Data trust.**
- General sentiment on r/Garmin that device-to-app sync becomes unreliable after firmware updates; "someone tell me: has Garmin always been this bad or is something going on recently?" — top fēnix 7 forum thread.

**Notifications / engagement.**
- The redesign removed color-coded goal wins. "The vibrant color coding that signaled goal achievements is gone."

**Onboarding.**
- Beta testers: "our feedback during the testing phase appears to have been largely disregarded."

**Subscription.**
- Garmin Connect+ (2025): community furious because Garmin was "the last bastion of no subscriptions" (T3). DC Rainmaker's Connect+ walkthrough thread became a forum for "fix bugs before charging."

**What Garmin does RIGHT (keep):**
- Unmatched raw metric breadth (power curve, HRV-status, training readiness, acute/chronic load).
- Course creation and turn-by-turn on device.
- Training Status algorithm is respected even when presentation isn't.
- Multi-sport recognition with minimal configuration.
- Workouts push reliably from calendar → device.
- Body Battery is a beloved at-a-glance concept (execution aside).
- Segments and PRs are well integrated.
- Strong ecosystem: Connect IQ, sensors, structured workouts.

### Whoop

Whoop's core value prop — recovery guidance — lands hard when it works and collapses when it doesn't.

**Data trust.**
- "After setting up a Whoop MG, the wearable mysteriously became unresponsive hours later" (engadget). The MG launch had a bug severe enough Whoop issued replacements.
- "Scores can feel like a black box, especially when they swing from one day to the next" (blog.melissau.com on Whoop recovery).

**Pricing / subscription.**
- Trustpilot: 3.2/5 "Average." Dominant complaint: the service is "too pricey" at ~$30/month vs. a $300 watch that does more.
- Whoop 5.0 launch (May 2025): $49 / $79 upgrade fees or a forced 12-month renewal after earlier "free upgrades at 6 months" promise. Reddit thread "Whoopgate: The Receipts." Bloomberg: "trending for all the wrong reasons." Whoop walked it back 24 hours later.
- 4.0 straps and apparel incompatible with 5.0: "a sales tactic."

**Actionable insights gap.**
- Recurring Reddit theme: people want guidance, not gauges. "Wanting more actionable guidance from their recovery data" is one of the three reasons users cite for leaving.
- Whoop measures cardiovascular strain, not muscular; this confuses lifters.

**Customer service.**
- "Slow to reply and hard to reach."

**What Whoop does RIGHT (keep):**
- Single-screen recovery-first daily view — clearest "here's your number today" of any product.
- The Journal (300+ tracked behaviors → correlation with outcomes) is a genuinely great pattern.
- Strain coach and target strain range — a rare "what should I do today?" prescription.
- Sleep debt and sleep coach are actionable.
- Screenless hardware means no UI distractions on wrist.
- Weekly/monthly performance assessments email is loved.

### Strava

Strava is still the social default; users love it, then complain about it, then don't leave.

**Scrolling / density.**
- The activity screen is well-regarded. The feed isn't. "Feed appeared very sparse despite following 250+ people."

**Paywall creep.**
- Year in Sport — free since 2016 — moved behind $79.99/yr paywall in Dec 2025 (Slashdot, T3, road.cc). "Strava has slowly been paywalling its features one piece at a time" (communityhub.strava.com, "Has usefulness of free subscription been throttled?").
- Community post title: "Strava, You Don't Have Rights To My Exercise Information. I Will NEVER Pay You for my own data."

**Social features broken.**
- Kudos limits (~20/day) on free accounts.
- Group kudos feature automatically giving kudos to all participants: "some found unhelpful and potentially creepy."
- Activity feed delay: "Significant Lag in Updates Since Monday" (community hub thread).
- "Activities selectively not being pushed to feed" — races missing while warmups appeared with identical privacy.
- AI slop: "AI-generated posts from new user accounts" with no activity history farming engagement.

**Data trust.**
- Occasional auto-pause mis-triggers, elevation wrong on loops, GPS drift recorded as segment PRs.

**Notifications.**
- Kudos push notification spam without good batching.

**What Strava does RIGHT (keep):**
- Segments. The single most imitated, never-bettered feature in endurance.
- Heatmaps (global, weekly, night, personal). Route discovery is Strava's moat.
- Kudos as low-friction acknowledgment — the right social primitive.
- Club/group feeds.
- Activity page map + elevation + split breakdown is a template.
- Route builder integration with heatmap.
- Year in Sport as ritual (even now paywalled).
- Photos on activities.
- Integration with every head unit.

### Oura

Oura's ring + app is the quietest of the bunch. When it works, users love it; when scores don't match felt experience, they bail.

**Data trust.**
- "The app often shows restful sleep when I barely got any" (Trustpilot / ouraring.com review).
- "Heart rate spikes during rest that skew daily readiness scores" (smartringsupport.com).
- "Workouts that vanish after syncing."
- Ring fit is the silent killer: "the most common cause of data discrepancies."

**Subscription.**
- After 2022 paywall: "Oura feels pointless without the subscription." Paying again "just to see data their ring already collected" (AppleInsider review title: "love the feature changes, hate the new subscription").
- BBB complaints about slow refund processing.
- An open-source app now exists so users can extract Oura data without the paywall — a significant signal.

**Activity tracking weak.**
- Activity Score under-weights volume vs. intensity; users who hit 10K steps with poor HRV get punished.
- Workout detection less reliable than wrist-worn peers.

**What Oura does RIGHT (keep):**
- Clean, minimal home screen with three scores: Sleep, Readiness, Activity. The "north-star trinity."
- Illness detection (temp trends) is genuinely loved.
- Period prediction is class-leading.
- Chronotype / sleep tag pattern detection.
- Readable sleep stages chart.
- Tagging activities (alcohol, caffeine, meditation) with outcome correlation — similar to Whoop Journal but simpler.
- Quiet, respectful notifications — not pushy.
- Long battery life and unobtrusive form factor.

### Apple Fitness / Apple Watch

Great for casuals, infuriating for cyclists.

**Cycling-specific failures.**
- "Cycled 65 minutes but exercise ring only showed 21 minutes" — Apple Community threads.
- "During leisurely cycling, coasting doesn't count."
- "VO2max calculations are specifically designed for walking and running and are not defined for other forms of exercise."
- iOS "is unable to automatically detect and track cycling activity" despite trivial signal.

**Data sync.**
- "Health kit and Apple fitness app don't appear to be fully connected."
- Cycling workouts from third parties (Peloton, etc.) silently fail to populate Fitness rings.

**UI / density.**
- "Fitness app is disorganized — both clutter and simplistic at the same time" (Apple Community).
- MacRumors thread: "The Apple Watch Activity Rings are pointless."

**What Apple does RIGHT (keep):**
- Rings concept. Three closed rings = one-glance daily success. Unbeaten visual device.
- Fitness+ coach-led workouts with Apple Watch metrics on-screen.
- Summary email / weekly summary cadence.
- Seamless share-rings social (without becoming a feed).
- "Close your rings" streaks.
- Haptic workout segmentation.
- Medication / cycle / hearing integrations.

### TrainingPeaks

Deep, coach-centric, unloved UI.

**Mobile / parity.**
- "Some functionality on the TrainingPeaks full website is not currently available in the mobile app" (official help center).
- "Users cannot use the Structured Workout Builder on mobile" — dealbreaker for athletes planning sessions on the go.
- UserVoice forum has 1465 open ideas. "Despite frequent updates, core features appear to be added infrequently."

**UI polish.**
- App Store reviews consistently call the UI dated.
- TrainingPeaks Virtual "occasionally hangs during structured workouts shortly after changing target power" (thezommunique).

**Onboarding.**
- PMC/CTL/ATL/TSB jargon wall. New users bounce.

**What TrainingPeaks does RIGHT (keep):**
- PMC chart (CTL/ATL/TSB). Every serious tool copies it.
- Peak Power/HR/Pace auto-detected and celebrated.
- Calendar drag-and-drop planning.
- Coach/athlete comment thread per workout — the right unit of communication.
- Structured workout push to Garmin/Wahoo.
- Post-workout RPE + feedback prompt.
- Performance Management Chart annotations.
- Season goals / event-date framing.

---

### Intervals.icu

Beloved for depth and free. Hated for density.

**Density / onboarding.**
- "Quite overwhelming at first glance" — common first impression.
- "Cycling metrics can be overwhelming; nothing intuitive" (intervals.icu forum).
- Users describe a "steep learning curve."

**UI polish.**
- "Not excellent" aesthetically. Charts are functional, not beautiful.
- "A few bugs, but understandable for a free solution."

**Positioning.**
- "Developers focus on cycling and power as KPI" — runners feel second-class.

**What Intervals.icu does RIGHT (keep):**
- Free. Donation-funded. Respected as independent.
- Depth rivals TrainingPeaks Premium.
- Calendar view + PMC + fitness/fatigue tracking.
- Wellness logging that correlates to performance.
- Automated test detection (FTP, VO2 estimates from rides).
- Imports from Strava, Garmin, etc. with minimal friction.
- Forum has direct developer engagement — community love this.
- Custom charts and data exploration.

## Cross-Cutting Theme Synthesis (Clustered by Complaint, Not Product)

### Theme A: "I can't find what matters today"
- Garmin: long scrolling lists of indistinguishable tiles.
- Intervals.icu: data firehose with no hierarchy.
- TrainingPeaks: same on mobile.
- Whoop: score given, context missing.
- Fix pattern: every screen needs one hero answer — "today's headline" — and progressive disclosure of supporting data.

### Theme B: "I don't trust the number"
- Oura readiness not matching lived state.
- Apple rings not closing on legit rides.
- Whoop black-box recovery swings.
- Strava segment ghost PRs from GPS drift.
- Fix pattern: show the inputs behind every score (contributors view). Expose confidence. Let users flag "this felt wrong" and learn from it.

### Theme C: "Stop charging me for my own data"
- Strava Year in Sport, Oura everything, Garmin Connect+, Whoop MG upgrade fees.
- Fix pattern: clear free core, clear premium enhancements, never retroactively paywall something users already had. Export-my-data must be frictionless.

### Theme D: "The social feed is decaying"
- Strava: sparse feeds, kudos limits, AI slop, selective activity display.
- Apple: no real social layer.
- Everyone else: no social worth mentioning.
- Fix pattern: if we do social, make it small, real (training partners, coaches), and honest. Chronological over algorithmic. Kudos-style primitives beat like/follow.

### Theme E: "Don't regress the update"
- Garmin v5 redesign stripped color signals; beta feedback ignored.
- Whoop 5.0 broke strap compatibility; reversed subscription policy in 24 hours.
- Fix pattern: ship ship to real cohort first; never remove a beloved feature without a replacement; color semantics are precious.

### Theme F: "The coach isn't there"
- Whoop gives strain numbers but no daily prescription.
- Oura gives a readiness score with a one-line tip.
- Garmin shows training status but not "what to do at 7am tomorrow."
- TrainingPeaks requires a human coach.
- Fix pattern: the exact whitespace. An AI coach that takes readiness + calendar + goals and produces a concrete, editable session, with rationale. This is our moat.

### Theme G: "Mobile is a second-class citizen"
- TrainingPeaks desktop-only workout builder.
- Garmin web dashboards removed without mobile equivalent.
- Fix pattern: mobile-first, everything doable on phone, including plan edits and chat.

### Theme H: "Cycling is a special case, stop treating it like running-lite"
- Apple VO2max excludes cycling.
- Oura Activity Score punishes long low-intensity rides.
- Whoop strain unreliable on trainer without HR strap.
- Fix pattern: treat power, normalized power, IF, VI, and cadence as first-class. Respect indoor vs. outdoor vs. gravel vs. road.

## Mistakes We Must Not Repeat (Checklist)

- [ ] Do not ship a long homogeneous scrolling list of metric tiles. One hero insight at top.
- [ ] Do not expose more than 3 top-level nav destinations on mobile. Everything else is progressive disclosure.
- [ ] Do not invent a composite score without a "contributors" view explaining where it came from.
- [ ] Do not let readiness/recovery score persist when it contradicts user-reported state — let users correct and retrain.
- [ ] Do not remove a free feature after users came to rely on it. If we charge, it's for new surface area.
- [ ] Do not charge users to export their own data.
- [ ] Do not build an algorithmic feed. Chronological, small-group, opt-in.
- [ ] Do not put core features behind desktop-only. Mobile-first or don't ship.
- [ ] Do not ship a redesign that loses color semantics or visual wins. Users remember those.
- [ ] Do not treat cycling as a generic "workout" — respect power, structure, and indoor/outdoor context.
- [ ] Do not silently fail sync. Explicit sync status with timestamp + retry.
- [ ] Do not push notifications with no batching or user-controlled quiet hours.
- [ ] Do not use jargon in onboarding (CTL, ATL, TSB, HRV rMSSD) without a plain-English layer.
- [ ] Do not hide beta feedback. Show users what we changed based on their input.
- [ ] Do not let AI-generated content pollute social surfaces.

## Patterns That Earn Love (Checklist)

- [ ] A single daily headline ("Today: easy Z2, 90min. Why: HRV dip after yesterday's race.")
- [ ] Three-score "trinity" home screen (Whoop/Oura pattern): Readiness, Training, Recovery.
- [ ] Contributors view under every score — transparent inputs with sparklines.
- [ ] A "why this?" link on every prescription (AI rationale in plain language).
- [ ] Journal / tagging that correlates behaviors to outcomes (Whoop Journal, Oura tags).
- [ ] PMC / fitness-fatigue chart with annotations (TrainingPeaks).
- [ ] Segments + route discovery via heatmap (Strava) — most imitated feature ever.
- [ ] Rings / closed-goal visual (Apple) — one-glance success primitive.
- [ ] Post-workout RPE + one-tap feedback prompt.
- [ ] Calendar drag-and-drop plan (TrainingPeaks).
- [ ] Coach-athlete comment thread per workout (TrainingPeaks).
- [ ] Weekly summary email / recap cadence.
- [ ] Illness-flag / anomaly detection (Oura).
- [ ] Live segment / target power comparison during workout.
- [ ] Quiet, respectful notifications (Oura).
- [ ] Free data export, open API.
- [ ] Forum with visible developer engagement (Intervals.icu).
- [ ] Color semantics: green = good, amber = caution, red = risk — consistent everywhere.
- [ ] Undo on AI modifications (we already have this — differentiator).
- [ ] Explicit sync state ("Last synced 3m ago ✓ Garmin / 1h ago ⚠ Whoop").

## Implications for Our AI Cycling Coach

1. **Dashboard = one headline + trinity + prescribed workout + contributors-on-tap.** Not twenty tiles.
2. **Chat = the "so what" layer no competitor has.** Users are begging for "what do I do with this recovery score?" That's us.
3. **Transparency = our trust-builder.** Every AI recommendation shows inputs and rationale. Every score shows contributors.
4. **Cycling-native, cycling-first.** Power, IF, NP, cadence, indoor/outdoor as first-class. Respect gravel vs. road.
5. **Mobile-first, undoable, honest about sync state.** These are table stakes that everyone else fails at.
6. **No paywall bait-and-switch.** If we charge, it's for AI depth, not for the user's own data.
7. **Weekly recap ritual** (Strava Year-in-Sport + Apple weekly summary) — earn love for free.
8. **Journal / tagging that correlates** — cheap to build, high retention.
9. **Color-coded goal achievement** — Garmin removed it and people mourned. We keep it.
10. **Minimal, opt-in social** — coach/training-partner circle, not a global feed.
