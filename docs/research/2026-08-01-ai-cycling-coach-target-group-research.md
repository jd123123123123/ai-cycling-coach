# Target-Group Research: What Cyclists Actually Need From an AI Coaching App

**Date:** 2026-08-01 (audited revision)
**Scope:** Seven rider segments, per-product pain points, Kano classification, prioritised build recommendations.
**Audience:** Engineers and product decision-makers choosing what to build next quarter.
**Language note:** The primary market is German-speaking. The report is in English because the codebase is. Every source in the corpus is English-language — this is the single largest gap in the research and is stated again in §2 and §8.

---

## 1. Executive summary — the five things that matter most

**1. The category's unsolved problem is legible causality, not better algorithms.** Across five platforms and five years, the most repeated complaint pattern is some form of "there's no feedback as to why." A user watching his projected FTP fall every week: "the prediction is either pure fantasy, or I'm doing something that's making me miss the prediction. But there's no feedback as to why" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-03](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/111842) — *unaudited*). The same complaint in 2021: "I have no idea what adaptations it's trying to suggest and why?" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2021-11](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/65676) — *the source data quoted a paraphrase as verbatim; the sentence above is the actual page text, and one 2021 thread cannot establish on its own that the complaint is "durable and multi-year"*). The terminal behaviour is not complaint but silent disablement: "I've pretty much given up with the AI now" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-05](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/113543) — *unaudited*), "execute and see it fall. I turned it off" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/111842) — *unaudited*). That retains the subscription and kills the product.

Important counterweight, and it changes the engineering brief: explanation is **hygiene, not magic**, and more is not linearly better. An N=216 within-subjects experiment found trust and understanding "rose from low to medium transparency but plateaued at high levels, suggesting excessive information can overwhelm the user, leading to distrust" — and in healthcare scenarios specifically, trust *declined* at maximum transparency ([Sullivan & Weger, HFES Proceedings, 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10711813251369473) — *unaudited*). Ship one sentence by default, with drill-down on demand.

**2. Unrequested overrides of a decision the athlete already made produce the corpus's most violent language.** A subscriber leaving after 14 years: "When I go into a system and pick a workout, the absolute last thing I want is for a DIFFERENT workout to be immediately synced out to my devices" — he calls it "arrogance" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-02](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/110882)). **Audit status: overstated.** The source data called this "the top churn trigger for experienced users"; the page is one departing user's post and establishes no such ranking (source supports a narrower claim). The *mechanism* is well evidenced and the design rule follows regardless: the engine proposes, the athlete disposes, and nothing syncs to a device the athlete did not pick. The same coin from the other side: "we're expected to pay for a system that needs to be manually overridden constantly and hand held by the users to get to work correctly" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-01](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/im-confused-about-my-plan/108763) — *supported*).

**3. Interruption competence is the widest cheap gap, and it is a strategy gap rather than a technology gap.** Doing the prescribed work on a different day *lowers* the athlete's progression levels, because the untouched original reads as a skip ([TrainerRoad forum, 2023-08](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/moving-workouts-on-calendar-and-adaptation/85819) — *unaudited*). The sanctioned fix after a missed week is manual calendar surgery ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-10](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/training-plan-adaptations-to-time-off/97325) — *unaudited*), with restarting the plan available as a fallback ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/pushing-rest-of-plan-forward-missed-a-week-of-training/68203) — **overstated**: staff do say "starting over completely is totally fine!", but as a fallback *after* the Illness-annotation route failed for that user, not as the primary official answer; the "destroys accumulated progress" consequence is the analyst's inference, not the thread's). On declared future absence the leader's position is explicit and reasoned — workouts across the declared window *are* removed, but "We intentionally _don't_, however, adapt away recovery weeks," because travel is itself a stressor ([TrainerRoad forum, 2025-03](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/is-adaptive-training-plans-really-adaptive/101840) — the claim that forward-declared unavailability is *unserved* is **overstated**; it is partially served, with a stated design rationale). That makes this a deliberate incumbent choice, which is exactly why it is worth a product cycle of lead.

**4. Sell the day, not the season — event-day rehearsal is the only place in the corpus where users are visibly doing the work by hand.** Riders build pacing plans from "estimate finish time, pick an IF by duration, multiply by FTP," with no segment or aid-station breakdown ([Gear & Grit, 2020-07](https://gearandgrit.com/how-to-build-a-pacing-plan-for-your-next-gravel-cycling-event/) — *unaudited*), trade hand-written course descriptions in forum threads, and self-seed so badly that "less than 10% actually did" finish under their predicted time ([TrainerRoad forum, 2025-05](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/unbound-gravel-2025/97735?page=15) — *unaudited*). The physiological case for replacing fresh-state FTP here is strong: after a ~4 h ride, 20-min and 6-min time-trial performance fell ~2.9% and ~10% at group mean — but "a range of ~1%–31% was observed between individuals" ([Hunter et al., *Experimental Physiology* 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576026/) — *unaudited for this sub-finding; the companion finding from the same paper on supra-threshold work is* **supported**). Even the incumbent concedes the gap: TrainerRoad says you can build all the fitness you need indoors, while separately recommending outdoor work for "technical skills and handling experience" ([TrainerRoad blog](https://www.trainerroad.com/blog/how-to-train-for-dirty-kanza/) — *unaudited*). Fitness and course-readiness are two problems; only one is solved.

**5. Do not ship a composite readiness score as a daily directive.** This is the strongest *negative* recommendation in the corpus — and also one of the most contested (see §8.1). Marco Altini's structural critique: compositing "mixes your physiology and behavior, giving you the false impression that is more insightful, while in fact, diluting the insight," and "the remaining useful information can only come from you, via a questionnaire" ([HRV4Training, 2024-02](https://hrv4training.substack.com/p/week-8-physiology-heart-rate-hrv) — *unaudited*). DC Rainmaker on Whoop recovery: "it has the least correlation between my actual feelings and what the score says, of most of these devices" ([DC Rainmaker, 2021-11](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2021/11/whoop-platform-review.html) — *supported, but this is a 2021 review of Whoop 4.0 and is five years stale for a claim about current reputational risk*). The credibility kill-shot is behavioural: "red means stop, except for when you don't and go anyway, and then still get a green the next day. At that point, I turned it off" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-03](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/red-light-green-light-rlgl-initial-thoughts-for-discussion/91699) — *supported*). Ship readiness as dated context with a visible input list, never as a gate.

## 2. Method and its limits

### How sources were gathered

Eight research angles were run in parallel, each targeting one segment or cross-cutting theme: ambitious amateurs (8–15 h/week); time-crunched working athletes (5–8 h/week); indoor/Zwift riders and racers; gravel and gran fondo event riders; masters 45+; triathletes using cycling-capable tools; women cyclists and cycle-aware coaching; and a cross-cutting sweep on readiness integration, nutrition, trust in AI, and subscription retention.

Sources are primary wherever possible:

- **Vendor community forums** — TrainerRoad, Xert, Intervals.icu, Wahoo, Zwift, Garmin (the largest source class by volume).
- **App Store review text** — JOIN, Athletica, Wild.AI, FitrWoman.
- **Independent long-form reviews** — DC Rainmaker, road.cc, Zwift Insider, Flies and Bikes, the5krunner.
- **Coaching publications** — CTS/TrainRight, TrainingPeaks coach blog, Uphill Athlete, FasterSkier, High North, EndureIQ, Roadman Cycling, CycleCoach.
- **First-person accounts** — one Unbound 200 race report, one age-grouper's self-experiment on carbohydrate tolerance.
- **Peer-reviewed sport science and HCI** — including four meta-analyses/systematic reviews, two RCTs, and one large in-situ elite-athlete monitoring study.

### How claims were audited

Every finding was required to carry (a) a verbatim quote or specific quantified result, (b) a resolvable source URL, and (c) a confidence rating. An **adversarial auditor then re-fetched source pages and judged each claim against the actual page content** — checking that quotes were verbatim rather than paraphrased, that superlatives and rankings were supported, that populations matched (elite vs recreational; cyclists vs lifters vs runners), and that the page did not contradict the claim it was cited for.

### The counts, honestly

| Bucket | Count | What it means for this report |
|---|---:|---|
| Raw findings gathered | 196 | — |
| **Cut as dead or unsupported** | **2** | Removed entirely; both described below |
| Kept | 194 | — |
| — `supported` | 55 | Auditor re-fetched the page and the claim holds at full strength. Only these may carry a headline claim alone. |
| — `overstated` | **27** | Auditor re-fetched and found the page supports something **narrower or weaker**. **These were retained in weakened form, not cut.** Each appears below at reduced strength with the correction visible inline. |
| — `unaudited` | 112 | Nobody re-checked the page. Used freely, but never as the sole support for a headline recommendation. |

**The overstated bucket is the most important line in this section.** 27 of 194 kept findings — 14% — were asserted more strongly than their source allows, and they were *weakened rather than removed* because the underlying mechanisms are usually real. The failure modes clustered tightly and will recur in any future research pass:

1. **Unsupported superlatives.** "The top churn trigger", "the #1 trust destroyer", "the single biggest unmet need", "the dominant failure mode", "the biggest hole" — each resting on one forum thread or one race report. A forum thread is evidence that a failure mode *exists and matters to real people*. It is never evidence of relative frequency.
2. **Paraphrase presented as quotation** ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/65676)).
3. **Population drift.** A finding cited as evidence about women cyclists is n=5 recreational *strength-training* women aged 24–32 ([Frontiers, 2025-02](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1519825/full)). A "long-course" triathlon result is actually a 5000 m run ([PLoS One, 2025-08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12349246/)).
4. **Pages that contradict their own citation.** The Garmin Training Readiness thread ends with the OP ignoring low readiness, training anyway, and reporting a fever ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/garmin-low-training-readiness-move-workout-or-skip/79182)). The Athletica reviewer cited for "startups lose users to instability" renewed his subscription ([Flies and Bikes](https://www.fliesandbikes.com/index.php/athletica-ai-long-term-review)). The intervals.icu client-confidence thread resolves with the fitness line being *correct* ([Intervals.icu forum](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/104874)).
5. **Invented precision.** "FTP estimation regularly overshoots by 15–20%" — the two overshoots on the cited page are +26% and +7% ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/ai-ftp-detection-too-high/110861)).
6. **Vendor marketing cited as third-party evidence.** The claim that free analytics is "actively displacing paid subscriptions" was sourced partly to the free product's own homepage ([intervals.icu](https://www.intervals.icu/)).
7. **Fabricated supporting detail.** A heat-acclimation finding asserted a "90°F+ race day"; the rider's own words are "even though it wasn't that hot," with conditions reported as "mid 80's" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/unbound-gravel-2025/97735?page=25)).

### The two cut findings

- A claim that Plan Builder prescribes more hours than the athlete declared available. **Cut:** the athlete selected the 3–7 h band and received 5:30–7 h; the "0–3 hours" figure he quoted was training history, not availability, and he accepted the explanation in-thread.
- A claim that vendors have stated a *technical* reason cycling-first tools cannot model run/swim load. **Cut:** the explanation came from a forum user, not the vendor, and the claim's entire weight rested on the word "vendor-stated."

Neither appears anywhere below, and neither should be reintroduced from memory.

### Known limits, stated plainly

- **Selection bias toward complaint.** Forums are where unhappy users go. Silent satisfied majorities are invisible here. Where a feature has both defenders and detractors, both appear — nine findings are explicitly flagged DISCONFIRMING and contradict the thesis of their own research angle.
- **Forum-specific selection bias.** The TrainingPeaks-stagnation evidence was collected on the *intervals.icu* forum — i.e. from people already migrating away ([Intervals.icu forum, 2025-01](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/86465)). App Store reviews are self-selected and unverifiable.
- **TrainerRoad over-representation.** Roughly half the forum sources are TrainerRoad, which has the most active public forum, not necessarily the most defects. Read "the category does X" as "the platform with the loudest forum does X, and where corroborated, so do others."
- **No German-language sources. Zero.** Nothing here establishes that German-speaking riders share these priorities, price sensitivities, or event culture. Ötztaler / Rad-Marathon / RTF culture and German-market pricing norms are entirely absent. **Treat every prioritisation in §7 as needing local validation before it becomes a roadmap.**
- **Staleness.** Several sources are 2–6 years old: the Zwift-vs-structured adherence thread is from 2018, the Garmin multi-device thread from 2023 and locked, the Whoop review from 2021, the ZRS analysis from Aug 2024 when ZRS was still in testing. Age is flagged inline where it matters.
- **Confidence is claim-level, not truth-level.** "High confidence" means the quoted evidence is unambiguous, not that the underlying belief is correct. Several high-confidence *user beliefs* are contradicted by high-confidence *physiology* — that tension is §8, not a footnote.

## 3. Must-have functions per target group

Seven groups. "Must-have" here means: absence causes rage, churn, or silent feature-disablement. These are not differentiators.

### 3.1 Ambitious amateurs, 10–15 h/week, self-coached, racing or fast group rides

1. **No unrequested overrides.** Every automated change is a proposal until accepted; nothing syncs to a head unit the athlete did not choose ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/110882) — *overstated as a churn ranking; the mechanism is sound*).
2. **A specific one-line cause on every plan change and every estimate move.** The failure state is "I have no idea what adaptations it's trying to suggest and why?" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/65676) — *overstated: single 2021 thread, and the source data paraphrased the quote*).
3. **Unstructured outdoor and group rides ingested, scored and credited.** This is their default riding mode. Vendor-admitted as unsolved since April 2021: "We are actively working on unstructured rides and workouts being accounted for a possible adaptation!" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2021-04](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/adaptive-training-problem/56757) — *supported*).
4. **Bounded, revertible threshold estimation** — see §4 and the Kano table; the damage is the cascade, not the number.
5. **Real periodisation control, not one aggressiveness slider.** Users report it as binary: set to aggressive it "adds a silly amount of intensity that would burn anyone out" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2025-06](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/has-adaptive-training-plateaued-my-progress-a-data-backed-review-with-help-from-ai/104192) — *unaudited for the slider sub-claim; the plateau finding on the same page is* **supported**).
6. **Ratio and durability metrics as an alternative progress story.** This segment already judges improvement by efficiency factor, decoupling and power:HR, and explicitly demotes FTP: "i never worry about FTP although at one point i thought i had to" ([Intervals.icu forum, 2024-02](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/60693) — *unaudited*).

### 3.2 Time-crunched working athletes, 5–8 h/week (parents, shift workers, travellers)

1. **Forward-declared unavailability absorbed into the plan maths before it happens.** "I was hoping that when I put it on the calendar it would reconfigure the rest days and workouts to fit my schedule" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/is-adaptive-training-plans-really-adaptive/101840) — **overstated**: the incumbent *does* remove workouts across declared time off and deliberately preserves the recovery week, so the gap is narrower than "unserved").
2. **Move any session to any day with zero penalty.** Current behaviour penalises the one coping strategy this group must use ([TrainerRoad forum, 2023-08](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/moving-workouts-on-calendar-and-adaptation/85819) — *unaudited*). Coach-side corroboration: the first listed reason athletes skip sessions is "They couldn't do the workout on the day you had it planned and didn't know how to adjust the plan to the reality of their schedule" ([TrainingPeaks coach blog](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/athletes-skip-workouts-improve-compliance/) — *unaudited*).
3. **Never "start over."** Always a graceful re-entry ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/pushing-rest-of-plan-forward-missed-a-week-of-training/68203) — *overstated as "the official escape hatch"; it is a stated fallback*).
4. **Do nothing structural after 1–2 missed sessions — and say so.** Carmichael: for one or two missed workouts, "just move on with your training like nothing happened"; cramming "generates so much fatigue you have to take more days off later" ([CTS/TrainRight, 2025-03](https://trainright.com/missed-workouts-guide-to-adjusting-training/) — *unaudited for this rule; the companion "be kind to yourself" finding from the same article is* **supported**). Pulford agrees: "If it's an isolated incident, I almost always let it go and just move forward" ([CTS/TrainRight, 2026-03](https://trainright.com/time-crunched-training-week-framework/) — *supported*).
5. **Ask before imposing a recovery week after time off.** Both defaults are wrong: users report "I 'missed' two weeks of interval training and now I find myself in a recovery week" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-06](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/no-plan-adaptations-after-a-week-off/93973) — *unaudited*), while the vendor rationale — time off is not always restful — is legitimate. Asking is the only correct resolution.
6. **Plan on max-sustainable-days-minus-one, anchored on two hard days, with 30/45/60-minute variants.** "Decide the maximum number of weekly training days you can handle, then subtract one"; "Two hard days per week is a great starting point"; and the accounting reality "A 90-minute ride is actually a 2-hour commitment, at minimum" ([CTS/TrainRight, 2026-03](https://trainright.com/time-crunched-training-week-framework/) — *supported*).

### 3.3 Indoor / Zwift racers

1. **Zwift races ingested as training and credited.** Users state the contradiction directly: "No point in calling it adaptive training if it doesn't 'know' how to read a unstructured ride" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-02](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/zwift-racing-and-adaptive-training/90701) — *unaudited*).
2. **Separate indoor zones.** 5–10% indoor-outdoor gap in habituated riders, 20–50 W in professionals, up to ~30% in recreational riders without fans; the advice is to test on the trainer and add 5–10 W outdoors ([High North Performance](https://www.highnorth.co.uk/articles/indoor-ftp-vs-outdoor-ftp) — *unaudited*).
3. **ERG-aware modelling.** ERG produces a materially lower HR at matched power than free riding or racing ([TrainerRoad forum, 2020-11](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/heart-rate-during-erg-workout-vs-race-free-ride/47317) — *unaudited*), so an ERG hour and a race hour are not the same stimulus.
4. **Explicit ERG-off / slope prescription for punchy work.** "Sprinting in Erg Mode is extremely counterproductive," ineffective under 20 seconds, and it "prevents surging and settling, limiting race-specific efforts" ([Slowtwitch, Jordan Rapp, 2020-04](https://www.slowtwitch.com/training/to-erg-or-not-to-erg/) — *unaudited*).
5. **Short-duration power (30 s–5 min) developed and tracked** — that is what decides these races and what categorisation under-weights: "races are normally won by sprints, but sprint ability is not factored in" ([Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/ce-long-term-review/) — *unaudited*).
6. **Cadence-aware targets and an auto-bail** against the ERG spiral of death: "Your cadence will slow even more due to this increased resistance, which means the trainer will pile on more resistance… until you come to a grinding halt" ([Zwift Insider, 2025-09](https://zwiftinsider.com/erg-mode-in-zwift/) — *supported for the mechanism; "adherence-breaking" and the auto-bail implication are inference — the page calls it "torturous" but never links it to quitting a plan*).

### 3.4 Gravel & gran fondo single-A-event riders

1. **Automatic, correct taper into the dated event.** 8–14 days is the sub-band with the largest effect; volume cut 41–60%; intensity and frequency maintained (TT SMD −0.45, TTE SMD 1.28) ([Wang et al., *PLOS ONE* 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0282838) — *supported, with one nuance: the paper's headline recommendation is a ≤21-day taper, and 8–14 days is the optimum within it*).
2. **Long-ride progression defensible against the event.** A 19-week plan for a 245 km race whose "longest ride in the 19 week plan is 2hrs!?" reads as the tool not understanding the event ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-05](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/training-plan-only-has-longest-ride-of-2-hrs/93263) — *supported, with two auditor caveats: this rests on a single thread, and respondents attribute it partly to the user's own availability settings plus a deliberate intensity-over-volume philosophy*). See §8.3 — whether long rides are *physiologically* required is genuinely contested.
3. **Event-appropriate energy systems.** "for a 2-hour steady effort, the focus should be on Tempo/Threshold. VO2 Max seems like the wrong energy system" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-01](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/feedback-on-plan-builder-tapering-recovery-and-event-specificity/108580) — *supported*).
4. **Camps and B-events absorbed into the fatigue model.** Currently: "TR doesn't seem to account for the fatigue generated here, as it has me scheduled for VO2 Max and Threshold work the very next week" ([same thread](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/feedback-on-plan-builder-tapering-recovery-and-event-specificity/108580) — *unaudited for this sub-claim*).
5. **Fuel and sodium planning rehearsed in training, as part of the plan.** Repeat finishers rank it above fitness: "pacing and hydration and fueling are way more important than keeping up on punch climbs" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-11](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/unbound-2025-plan/98319) — *unaudited*).

### 3.5 Masters cyclists 45+

1. **Prescription driven by observed recovery, training age and life stress — never by birthday.** The riders name the variable themselves: "Training age is much more of a factor than how many trips round the sun we've made" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/masters-plan-or-not/111438) — *unaudited*).
2. **Configurable work:rest ratios (2:1, 3:1) and hard-day spacing as user-owned settings.** "I have found that I now need to have a 3 week on, 1 week recovery plan," and on the tool: "PB is not great for modifying schedules… masters type approach is included in that limitation" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2020-08](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/plan-builder-for-masters-age-athletes-feature-request/41152) — *supported*).
3. **Genuinely easy easy days.** "TR routinely schedules my z2/recovery rides too intense" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/adaptations-for-getting-older/81855) — *unaudited*).
4. **Five-day weeks as a first-class structure.** "6 days a week doesn't allow for real rest" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2020-01](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/recovery-for-older-athletes/28064) — *unaudited*).
5. **Strength work budgeted *against* interval days.** The top reported barrier is fatigue/soreness affecting endurance training (50.6%), ahead of time (36.2%) and motivation (21.6%); in-season participation drops from ~80% to ~60% ([JFMK, n=555 male masters cyclists, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11586982/) — *unaudited for the barrier figures; the bone-health finding from the same paper is* **supported**). One rider's arithmetic: "if I try to add lower body gym work I have to dial it back to 2x hard intervals per week" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/masters-plan-or-not/111438) — *supported, but it is a single post presented as a pattern*).
6. **Two hard days as a default, not a cap.** Multiple independent posters converge on "no more than two high intensity workouts a week" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/adaptations-for-getting-older/81855) — *supported*), and one coaching organisation specifies 48–72 h between hard sessions vs 24–48 h for younger athletes ([Uphill Athlete, 2026-07](https://uphillathlete.com/aerobic-training/the-5-2-framework-how-masters-athletes-should-structure-their-week/) — *supported as a quotation, but the auditor notes this is a single uncited coaching framework from a mountain-sport organisation; "coaching consensus" overstates one source*). See §8.4 — the physiology does not back this cleanly.

### 3.6 Triathletes (bike side only)

1. **A clean bike-only mode.** The longest-running merged feature request in the corpus, dating to 2019: "I have my own swim and run workouts and I want to add the bike portion" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2019-06](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/feature-request-bike-only-workouts-from-triathlon-plans/16716) — *supported*).
2. **Honest refusal on run/swim modelling rather than a bad estimate.** A 25-minute tempo run estimated at 50–54 TSS scored 74 in TrainingPeaks — roughly a 50% error ([DC Rainmaker, 2023-01](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2023/01/trainerroad-officially-launches-running-support-heres-how-it-works.html) — *supported for the measurement error; "systematically mis-measure" extrapolates from one reviewer's tests*). A commenter's consequence: "My bike sessions wouldn't change based on how well/poorly I did a run, so it was effectively useless for rides for me as well" ([same source](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2023/01/trainerroad-officially-launches-running-support-heres-how-it-works.html) — **overstated** as "the single hardest requirement triathletes name"; one commenter is not a segment-wide ranking).
3. **Bike session *type* chosen for its residual cost to running.** "Do a genuine 3x20 at threshold on Tuesday and your run sessions for the next three days are compromised" ([Roadman Cycling, 2026-04](https://roadmancycling.com/blog/triathlon-cycling-training-plan) — *supported, but this is coaching opinion asserting the tax with no study cited*).
4. **Low-volume bike plans as the design centre, not the compromise.** Athletes deliberately choose low volume and post 4:52 halves on 6–7 h/week ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/half-im-low-volume-perfomance/29210) — *unaudited*).

### 3.7 Women cyclists, including perimenopausal riders

1. **Menstrual cycle and heat as post-workout attribution reasons.** Shipped by TrainerRoad on 1 July 2026 — Heat for all athletes, Menstrual Cycle for non-male accounts — with the right framing: "A workout you cut short because you were overheating is not the same as one you cut short because you have lost fitness" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-07](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/new-survey-options-heat-menstrual-cycle/114167) — *supported*). **This is now table stakes, not a differentiator.**
2. **Hormonal contraceptive users as a first-class case.** OC use has an effectively trivial group-level performance effect (between-group ES 0.13–0.18; within-group "very close to zero"), and phase logic does not apply ([Elliott-Sale et al., *Sports Medicine* 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7497464/) — *unaudited*).
3. **Symptom logging that is optional and near-zero-effort.** In 20 UK competitive female cyclists, "50% tracked menstruation, but only basic onset notation—none systematically tracked symptoms" ([PLOS ONE, 2026-06](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0343892) — *unaudited*). A product demanding rich logging fights existing behaviour.
4. **Fix the basics the incumbents get wrong.** Garmin predicts "a period each week despite entering my cycle as 27 days" ([Garmin forums](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-ios/161057/menstrual-cycle-tracking) — the accompanying claim that there is *no way to correct an entry* is **overstated**: the thread resolves with users pointing to an "Edit Period" function, making this a discoverability failure, which is a weaker and different problem than a missing capability).

## 4. Common pain points with existing AI/adaptive coaching apps, per product

### TrainerRoad — Adaptive Training, AI FTP Detection, Red Light/Green Light, Plan Builder

The most-documented product here, and the most over-represented — read the volume as forum activity, not defect density.

- **Estimate overshoot cascades into unachievable sessions.** "My AI FTP detection bumped my FTP from 233 to 293," followed by failing a sweet-spot workout after one of three intervals ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-02](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/ai-ftp-detection-too-high/110861) — **overstated**: the source data asserted a "regular 15–20% overshoot"; the two documented overshoots are +26% and +7%, and nothing on the page establishes frequency). Compounding case: a +2.2 progression-level jump landing simultaneously with a 277→292 W bump ([same thread](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/ai-ftp-detection-too-high/110861) — *unaudited*).
- **A high-compliance athlete flipped to near-total failure by an update.** "I would normally expect only to fail a couple of workouts in an entire season" → "What IS new is TR AI and now - overnight - failing all hard sessions," and downgrading to conservative mode did not fix it ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-02](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/tr-ai-update-i-am-failing-my-workouts/111202) — *supported, n=1 and framed as such*).
- **Adaptations move opposite to lived experience.** "Nail a workout, feel great, projected FTP goes down and (importantly) future workouts get easier" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/111842) — *unaudited*). Exceeding targets and then being downgraded reads as broken ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-01](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/ai-adapting-my-next-ride-down-why/89598) — *unaudited*).
- **Readiness sees only training load.** "RLGL is pretty useless to me until it starts considering stress from non cycling & running workouts," with the warning that "if you blindly accepted its suggestions, it could actually be worse than not using it at all" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-03](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/red-light-green-light-rlgl-initial-thoughts-for-discussion/91699) — *unaudited*). Nobody knows how strength enters the model — users assume it "assumes you wrecked yourself every set" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-03](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/perplexed-by-the-latest-iteration-of-adaptive-training-and-ai/112298) — *unaudited*).
- **All-or-nothing adaptation diffs.** "we don't have the option to accept some but not all adaptations" ([same RLGL thread](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/red-light-green-light-rlgl-initial-thoughts-for-discussion/91699) — *unaudited*).
- **Explanation is refused as policy.** Users ask directly — "I would love an explanation to why the AI gave me a red light!" — and the vendor cites competitive risk in revealing the algorithm ([TrainerRoad forum, 2025-02](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/ive-got-a-red-light-and-i-cant-see-why/100836) — **overstated** as "the #1 trust destroyer": the demand and the refusal are both real, but the ranking is not, and the page shows users *debating* whether to override rather than systematically overriding).
- **Struggle surveys are over-prompted and mis-triggered.** "I've gotten three recently where I didn't struggle"; "There should be an option for 'I didn't struggle'"; one triggered by briefly stopping pedalling in a warmup ([TrainerRoad forum, 2022-09](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/are-the-struggle-surveys-more-easily-triggered-now-than-in-the-past/76031) — *unaudited*).
- **Outdoor rides silently corrupt the model.** Associating an outdoor ride with a scheduled workout means "TR just takes that as a 'check' and assumes you did the workout as prescribed, without major deviation" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2023-01](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/adaptive-plans-and-outside-rides/80079) — *supported for the mechanism; "the biggest hole" is an unranked superlative, and a moderator states Workout Levels V2 will assign credit for unstructured rides, so the gap may be closing*).
- **Plan Builder is phase-locked and needs manual repair.** "It's locked in to plan phases (base/build/speciality) and timing of recovery, and can't change these in an intelligent way"; "it took me another hour or two to manually fix several things that felt fundamentally wrong with the plan logic" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-01](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/feedback-on-plan-builder-tapering-recovery-and-event-specificity/108580) — *supported*).
- **Ambitious-volume riders report measurable plateau.** FTP flat 2021 → Jan 2025; 731 vs 591 TSS (a 19% cut); "the entire system is biased toward the less experienced athletes and time crunched athletes" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2025-06](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/has-adaptive-training-plateaued-my-progress-a-data-backed-review-with-help-from-ai/104192) — *supported; note the OP trains ~10 h/week, the bottom of the 10–15 h band*).
- **Triathlon plans are described as hazardous for beginners** — a "double black diamond": excellent for experienced athletes who modify them, destructive for beginners using them as written ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/triathlon-all-but-abandoned-by-tr/58688) — *unaudited*).

**DISCONFIRMING for TrainerRoad.** Automated FTP is genuinely popular with a cohort that hates ramp testing — "AI FTP is the best for setting my training FTP. I hate ramp tests"; "It absolutely works for me" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/ai-ftp-detection-comments-and-questions/106025) — *supported, though the auditor notes this is 3–4 posters in a ~20-post thread; "large cohort" is unquantified*). RL/GL also has genuine fans: "I found RL/GL very accurate, scarily so," and one user reports it visibly settling down after a couple of weeks of added volume ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-09](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/95847) — **overstated**: the first half is verbatim, but the conclusion drawn from it in the source data — that "the complaint is about opacity and staleness, not the concept" — is not on that page; that thread's complaints are about over-weighting outdoor rides).

### Xert

- **Advisor volatility.** Recommendations jumping from 30 XSS to 276 XSS within hours with no intervening activity; status flipping Tired → Fresh overnight with no rides between; and the verdict "The ATA is providing no clear actionable advice in this case" ([Xert forum](https://forum.xertonline.com/t/adaptive-training-advisor-nonsense/38241) — *unaudited*).

The product insight came from another user: "How about a pop-up message when you select a target date WAY TOO FAR into the future?" ([Xert forum, 2024-01](https://forum.xertonline.com/t/43482) — *unaudited*).
- **Outdoor workouts unexecutable.** "most if not all of my riding is out doors…most of the suggested rides are WAY too complicated," with the workaround being a head-unit data field rather than the plan ([Xert forum, 2025-01](https://forum.xertonline.com/t/46569) — *unaudited*).

### Intervals.icu

- **CTL/"Fitness" is distrusted by exactly the sophisticated users it should serve.** "It really just reflects riding volume… How has this become an acceptable metric in the cycling community?" — with a defender conceding it "overly weights time over intensity" ([Intervals.icu forum, 2025-09](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/111261) — *unaudited*).
- **The chart causes coaching-relationship friction — but less damningly than it first appears.** A coach reports a client seeing "his blue line have been consistently dropping" while believing he was "working really hard recently" ([Intervals.icu forum, 2025-06](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/104874) — **overstated**: the thread's own resolution undercuts "the chart destroys confidence." The fitness line was *correct*; the visual change came from the coach temporarily misconfiguring power zones, and the client's weekly load really had dropped ~50%. This is a data-literacy and communication failure, not a misleading chart).
- **UI density causes outright rejection, including by the target user.** "the consistent result was rejection due to the overwhelming UI"; the maintainer concedes the UX is "way too complex and inconsistent"; and the sharpest reframe: "The amount of information is not necessarily the problem, but rather the way it is organized" ([Intervals.icu forum, 2025-05](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/101779) — *unaudited*).
- **Weaker mobile experience** is the named retention anchor keeping people on TrainingPeaks ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-05](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/switching-from-training-peaks-premium-to-intervals-icu/93524) — *the mobile-anchor half is well supported; the source data's lead assertion that users switch primarily on price-for-value is* **overstated** *— the OP switched on feature parity, and price is not cited as the reason anywhere in that thread*).

### TrainingPeaks

- **Retained as infrastructure, not loved.** "completely stagnated in new features for 5 years"; "poor customer service and zero responses to suggestions for feature enhancements" — while retained for the archive back to 2009, the 80/20 plan library and the ATP ([Intervals.icu forum, 2025-01](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/86465) — *supported, with the auditor's selection-bias flag: this evidence was collected on a competitor's forum*).
- **Price rises land on a stagnation narrative.** Annual Premium $124.99 → $134.99 from 2 April 2025; coach tier +~16%. - **Analysis depth is misunderstood by buyers.** "TP is not similar to Xert or intervals.icu. It is a training diary…the real analytics are in WKO which is an entirely different product" ([Wahoo forum, 2023-06](https://wahoox.forum.wahoofitness.com/t/training-peaks-vs-intervals-icu-vs-xert/22977) — *unaudited*).

### Zwift

- **Built-in plans are static and give no feedback.** "Exceptionally boring Training Plan which teaches almost nothing"; typos and "mismatches between text and target cadence" persisting for years ([Zwift forums, 2024-11](https://forums.zwift.com/t/training-plans-reviewed/597501) — *unaudited*).
- **Users are asking Zwift to integrate rather than build.** Requests include "AI-driven workout plans," "Dynamically adjusted workout targets, like Xert's SMART workouts," wearable-driven daily adjustment, and "Zwift integrate with more training specific apps than make their own thing" ([Zwift forums, 2024-01](https://forums.zwift.com/t/training-plan-requests/620798) — *unaudited*).
- **Structured workouts and race/group events are mechanically incompatible**, and a segment reverted to running two apps after the official integration: "I've gone back to running zwift and TR separately on my PC even after the integration" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/trainer-road-workout-inside-a-zwift-event-or-race/105889) — *supported*).
- **Category/score opacity.** Riders "need to understand why they are placed in a certain pen"; heavier riders and those with big 30 s power feel seeded too high ([Zwift Insider, 2024-08](https://zwiftinsider.com/zrs-update-20240731/) — **overstated**: the claim that Category Enforcement "largely solved sandbagging" is not established on that page, which only notes sandbaggers now race stronger riders; and the page describes ZRS as still in testing in Aug 2024, so the grievance description may be two years stale). The machinery kept moving into Sep 2025: ZRS decay reintroduced with a 30-day grace period, instant ZRS raises on new power PBs, and "30-Day Max Score Enforcement" against score-tanking ([Zwift Insider, 2025-09](https://zwiftinsider.com/tsoz-closer-look-zrs-changes/) — *unaudited*).
- **Price shock.** UK monthly £12.99 → £17.99 in May 2024: "Just a basic 'f*** you, we're hiking prices, deal with it'…I'll see you in the winter"; "Bold move to increase prices by 40 per cent just as the weather is improving" ([road.cc, 2024-05](https://road.cc/content/news/zwift-accused-taking-users-granted-price-hike-308245) — *unaudited*). ### Wahoo SYSTM

- **Non-adaptivity is the stated switching reason.** "The training plans don't adapt if you miss a session or decide to ride outdoors instead of indoors," plus complaints that plans "now have a majority of No Vids, Inspiration videos or On Location - none of which keep me motivated" ([Wahoo forum, 2025-07](https://wahoox.forum.wahoofitness.com/t/lack-of-adaptive-training-makes-me-switch-from-systm/29548) — *unaudited*).

### Garmin

- **Daily Suggested Workouts degrade without a goal and cannot see life stress.** "without one, DSW operates in general fitness-maintenance mode rather than a periodised build toward a specific event," and "poor sleep, travel, occupational load — remain invisible to the algorithm unless they produce measurable changes in resting heart rate, HRV Status, or overnight Body Battery" ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/garmin-features/training/daily-suggested-workouts/) — *supported in this scoped form*). **The same source was cited elsewhere in the corpus for a stronger claim — that DSW is "purely reactive" with "no forward plan," degrading after "any gap" — and that version is** **overstated**: DSW *does* periodise when a race goal is set, and degradation follows extended inactivity pushing Training Status into detraining.

- **Multi-device inconsistency.** The Fenix downgraded a threshold workout to a base ride as the day progressed while the Edge kept the threshold suggestion ([Garmin forums, 2023](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/cycling/f/edge-840-series/338035/daily-suggested-workout-on-edge-840) — *supported, but the thread is locked and 3+ years old, describing a specific firmware era*).
- **Cycle tracking is barebones and disconnected.** "The widget only tells you which cycle day you're on and in which phase. That's kinda helpful-ish, but not amazing" — it feeds nothing into Body Battery or recovery ([Garmin forums](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/412522/menstrual-cycle-tracking-underwhelming) — *unaudited*).

- **Praised for unifying all three sports.** "finally there's a tri program that can take all my data, make sense of it, and tailor my training plan to it" — while acknowledging "a lot of bugs/quirks of AI" ([Apple App Store, 2025](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/athletica-ai-training-plans/id6737747559) — *supported, with the standard App Store self-selection caveat*).

- **Input credibility is attacked before the AI framing is.** Wrist-derived HRV for threshold determination called "sketchy" and a "GIGO service"; the founder conceded they "pressed pause on this integration" (DFA alpha-1) over reliability ([TrainerRoad forum, 2023-04](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/another-ai-training-app-athletica-ai/82882) — *unaudited*). ### Strava

- **Generic post-hoc AI summaries are mocked, not merely ignored.** "the AI feedback lacks any sort of real purpose"; after a 10 km PB, the revised prediction was slower than the time just run ([road.cc, 2025-08](https://road.cc/content/feature/strava-worth-paying-315565) — *unaudited*). The viral failure: after a rider was hit by a car door, the AI replied "Ouch, hope you're okay after that car accident! Despite the setback, your activity data shows you're a consistent, well-rounded athlete—keep up the great work!" **This is the failure mode an LLM-based coach drifts into by default, which makes it the single most important thing to design against.**

### Wild.AI and FitrWoman (the cycle-aware niche)

- **Wild.AI's dominant complaints are technical, not conceptual.** Login failures, data not saving, crashes, wearable connection failures ("I couldn't connect my oura ring (or any other wearable)"). ## 5. Underserved needs and differentiation opportunities

Eleven theses, ordered roughly by how much of the corpus supports them. Each names a mechanism, not just a gap.

**T1 — Legible causality as a product primitive, delivered at variable depth.** Every platform fails identically: the adaptation is visible, the intent is not. But because trust plateaus — and in health contexts declines — at maximum transparency ([Sullivan & Weger](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10711813251369473)), the answer is a one-sentence default plus on-demand drill-down: an **adaptation ledger** recording what changed, on what evidence, and what would reverse it. **Critical engineering constraint:** the explanation layer must be wired to the actual decision record. An LLM that plausibly narrates a decision it did not observe is worse than no explanation, because it is unfalsifiable — and it is exactly how you end up producing Strava-grade output ([road.cc](https://road.cc/content/feature/strava-worth-paying-315565)). Also note that legibility is *functional*, not differentiating: explanation removes rage, it does not create advocacy.

**T2 — Interruption competence, with a counterintuitive asymmetry.** Adapt aggressively to *declared future* absence; do essentially nothing after 1–2 *past* misses ([CTS/TrainRight](https://trainright.com/missed-workouts-guide-to-adjusting-training/)). Most teams will build the opposite, because reshuffling after a miss looks more "adaptive." The incumbent's position on declared time off is a stated design choice rather than a capability gap ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/is-adaptive-training-plans-really-adaptive/101840)), which is what makes it defensible for a cycle.

**T3 — Event-day rehearsal.** The only place in the corpus where users are visibly doing the work by hand: pacing spreadsheets, peer-traded course knowledge, bad self-seeding, heat they never trained in, fuelling that collapsed at hour four ([TrainerRoad pre-race thread](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/unbound-gravel-2025/97735?page=15), [Gear & Grit](https://gearandgrit.com/how-to-build-a-pacing-plan-for-your-next-gravel-cycling-event/), [Caroline Worrall race report](https://carolineworrall.substack.com/p/unbound-gravel-200-2025) — *the race report is* **supported** *for the mechanism, though "the dominant failure mode" is a generalisation one report cannot carry*). Unexpected, emotionally loaded, tied to the one day the season was for, and structurally awkward for a plan-library incumbent to bolt on.

**T4 — Replace the fitness chart with durability.** CTL is distrusted by exactly the users you want ([Intervals.icu](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/111261)) and FTP is explicitly demoted by the same people ([Intervals.icu](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/60693)). Durability has both the best physiological story and the best *personalisation* story: the 1–31% between-athlete range means a durability profile is genuinely per-athlete rather than a rescaled group curve ([Hunter et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576026/)). It also explains why surges wreck the back half — "even small amounts of work above CP (2.5 kJ kg−1) resulted in greater decreases of maximal mean power and CP when compared with work-matched exercise performed below CP" ([same paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576026/) — *supported, noting this is a review relaying Mateo-March et al., and CP is not "threshold" loosely construed*).

**T5 — Treat real-world riding as first-class input, then say what it was.** Hygiene level: ingest and credit group rides, commutes and Zwift races. Delight level: name the ride physiologically and act — "Saturday's club run was your VO2 session; Tuesday becomes endurance." Riders already do this reasoning manually and badly: mapping race types onto workout slots, tagging races as C-events, guessing TSS, dropping to lower-volume plans to free a day ([TrainerRoad, 2020](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/which-workouts-to-replace-with-zwift-races/34525) — *supported*; [TrainerRoad, 2021](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/zwift-racing-and-adaptive-training-plan-builder/66576) — *unaudited*). Unlike a readiness score, this statement is falsifiable, which is why it will be believed.

**T6 — Compete on a trust *contract* for estimates, not on estimate accuracy.** The complaints about auto-FTP are never really about the number — they are about the cascade and about direction-of-travel contradicting lived experience. The sceptical segment supplies the workable bar itself: "I don't care if the Vo2max is accurate, only care if it's self consistent" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-07](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/94984) — *unaudited*). Nobody in this market makes a *checkable promise about estimator behaviour* — bounded step size, corroborating effort named, one-tap revert, full change history. Accuracy claims are unfalsifiable and therefore worthless; a behavioural guarantee is neither.

**T7 — Serve the ambitious 10–15 h/week rider that safety-tuned engines under-serve, and say so out loud.** The complaint is quantified and structural ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/has-adaptive-training-plateaued-my-progress-a-data-backed-review-with-help-from-ai/104192) — *supported*), and the escape hatch is binary. Real periodisation control with a stated ceiling and a stated reason is positioning no incumbent can adopt without admitting their default is conservative. *Caveat honestly: the primary source trains ~10 h/week, the bottom of the claimed band, so the top of the range is inference.*

**T8 — Anti-differentiation is half the strategy.** Four things the market's "AI" energy flows into that the evidence says burn trust: composite readiness scores that gate workouts; generic post-hoc AI ride commentary; four-phase menstrual periodisation templates; and AI-as-human-coach-replacement positioning ("I don't feel personally accountable to a text box the same way I feel personally responsible to a coach. The value of a coach is not in creating a training plan" — [TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/98421), *supported*). Refusing to ship these is cheaper than shipping them and is itself a marketable stance.

**T9 — Nutrition is the highest-variance surface: biggest delighter and biggest liability in the same subsystem.** Delight lives in gut training (a progressive carbohydrate ladder that finds the *individual* ceiling — one age-grouper documented a reproducible cliff between 105 and 120 g/h, with 4/5 GI distress by hour 2.5 at 120 ([prommer.net, 2026-04](https://prommer.net/en/training/nutrition/120g-carb-arms-race/) — *unaudited*)), in sodium as an independently tuned variable (riders self-testing at 1000–1600 mg/h, far above default sports-drink assumptions — [TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/unbound-gravel-2025/97735?page=15), *supported*), and in heat-adjusted fuelling. Liability lives in prescribing pro carbohydrate numbers to amateurs who actually eat 22–85 g/h ([TrainerRoad forum, 2026-02](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/real-life-normal-person-carbs-per-hour-on-the-bike/110940?page=2) — *unaudited*), and in any weight- or deficit-first framing (see the Kano reverse rows).

**T10 — Position on responsiveness; price with choice rather than nerve.** The only feature in the corpus that by itself moved a paying customer off a competitor was a developer being responsive: "I like intervals.icu better and actually cancelled my TP subscription… David at intervals.icu is very responsive to customer needs" ([Wahoo forum](https://wahoox.forum.wahoofitness.com/t/training-peaks-vs-intervals-icu-vs-xert/22977) — *unaudited*). The mirror image is TrainingPeaks ([Intervals.icu forum](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/86465) — *supported*). On pricing: churn here is life-stage driven and explicitly recoverable — "I used to love TrainerRoad. I'll be back" ([TrainerRoad forum, 2024-08](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/95358) — *supported*) — and TrainerRoad's optional-price move generated goodwill, with users volunteering to pay *more* ([DC Rainmaker, 2023-01](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2023/01/trainerroad-raises-optional.html) — *unaudited*).

**T11 — Where this app already stands.** Stated plainly, because it changes what is worth building:
- **At parity — all hygiene, none differentiating:** adaptive plans, PMC/load tracking, FTP estimation, ride analysis.
- **Genuine asset, under-exploited:** the tool-using AI coach chat. It is the only architecture in the market that can deliver variable-depth explanation on demand and satisfy the curvilinear-transparency finding — *if* it reads a decision record rather than narrating post hoc.
- **Likely behind, all cheap:** interruption handling, unstructured-ride credit, per-session verdict, separate indoor zones, holiday/camp absorption in the load model, cycle/heat attribution logging, non-7-day weeks.
- **Liabilities already owned:** PMC/CTL as the headline progress metric, recovery trend as a composite score, and the whole nutrition surface. The two most "finished-looking" features are the two most likely to lose trust with the target segment.

## 7. Prioritised feature recommendations per target group

P0 = build next quarter. P1 = the quarter after, or cheap enough to slot in. P2 = only after validation.

**Cross-cutting P0 that unblocks everything else: an adaptation ledger.** Every automated change writes a structured record — what changed, the inputs that caused it, the confidence, and what would reverse it. One sentence renders by default; the AI chat reads the ledger for drill-down. This is the substrate for T1, T5, T6 and every "why" complaint in §4, and it is the only way the chat can explain decisions without confabulating.

### 7.1 Ambitious amateurs, 10–15 h/week
- **P0** — Proposal-only adaptations with per-item accept/reject; nothing syncs to a head unit unrequested.
- **P0** — Unstructured/group ride ingestion with progression credit.
- **P0** — Estimate trust contract: bounded step size, named corroborating effort, one-tap revert, visible change history.
- **P1** — Durability panel (power at hour 3/4/5, decoupling, EF trend) promoted above CTL on the progress surface.
- **P1** — High-ceiling mode: explicit periodisation controls with a stated load ceiling and a stated reason, replacing the single aggressiveness slider.
- **P2** — "What was that ride?" classification that cancels the redundant midweek session (requires the ledger and ingestion first).

### 7.2 Time-crunched working athletes, 5–8 h/week
- **P0** — Forward-declared unavailability: calendar blocks consumed by the planner *before* the window, with the reshape explained.
- **P0** — Move-without-penalty: reconcile completed work against intent regardless of calendar slot.
- **P0** — Miss policy that does nothing structural for 1–2 sessions and *says* it is doing nothing ("that cost you almost nothing — here's why"), citing the 30%-volume-cut evidence.
- **P1** — Ask-don't-assume prompt after a gap: "was that week restful or stressful?" — one tap, feeds the recovery-week decision.
- **P1** — 30/45/60-minute variants generated for every prescribed session; plan built on max-days-minus-one, anchored on two hard days.
- **P2** — Date-relative (non-Monday-Sunday) planning, which incidentally unlocks shift rotations.

### 7.3 Indoor / Zwift racers
- **P0** — Race ingestion: Zwift races recognised, scored, credited, and the now-redundant session removed.
- **P0** — Separate indoor/outdoor thresholds; ERG vs free-ride flagged on every session so the load model does not equate them.
- **P1** — Race-prep sessions prescribed ERG-off/slope with the reason stated; cadence-aware targets and an auto-bail.
- **P1** — 30 s–5 min power tracked as a first-class profile alongside threshold, with an explanation of which duration is limiting race outcomes.
- **P2** — Post-race recovery guidance sized to what the race actually cost, plus aerobic-volume protection across race-heavy blocks (a racer's own 313-race year raised eFTP 298→323 W while long rides "all but disappeared" — [Zwift Insider, 2026-01](https://zwiftinsider.com/300-races/), *supported, n=1 self-report*).
- **Pricing note:** position as a cheap companion layer, not a second full platform — combined ~$42/month already deterred adoption ([DC Rainmaker, 2025-02](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2025/02/trainerroad-zwift-integration-finally-arrives-full-details.html) — *unaudited*).

### 7.4 Gravel & gran fondo event riders
- **P0** — Automatic taper into the dated event (8–14 d, −41–60% volume, hold intensity and frequency).
- **P0** — Event-demand-driven specificity: no VO2/anaerobic blocks in the weeks before a sustained sub-threshold event; camps and B-events consumed by the fatigue model.
- **P1** — Course-and-day rehearsal v1: segment-level pacing from route data, aid-station splits, expected power decay, and a fuel/sodium schedule. **This is the marketing spearhead, not a side feature.**
- **P1** — Durability-derived pacing target (hour-6 capability) rather than fresh FTP × an IF picked by duration.
- **P2** — Heat acclimation block keyed to the event's historical conditions — noting the corrected finding that acclimation failure bit at *mid-80s°F*, not extreme heat.
- **P2** — Gut-training ladder (progressive carb g/h with GI logging). High delight, high liability; do not ship without fuel-adequacy-first framing.

### 7.5 Masters 45+
- **P0** — No age branch. Ever. Prescription driven by observed recovery, training age and declared life stress.
- **P0** — User-owned work:rest ratio (2:1, 3:1) and minimum hard-day spacing, with the rationale shown and the ability to tighten *or* loosen.
- **P1** — Genuinely easy easy days: cap prescribed Z2 intensity and verify against executed data.
- **P1** — Strength sessions budgeted against interval days in the same weekly load model, with soreness carry-over modelled.
- **P2** — Volume-consistency framing in copy ("don't let volume slide") rather than decline framing — 54% of male / 39% of female masters VO2max decline variance is explained by training-volume change ([CycleCoach, 2026-06](https://www.cyclecoach.com/blog/2026/6/29/why-masters-cyclists-lose-fitness-faster-and-what-the-research-says-to-do-about-it) — *supported, but this is a coaching blog restating a primary study it does not name; cite the underlying paper before this number goes into the product*).

### 7.6 Triathletes (bike side only)
- **P0** — A clean bike-only mode, and an explicit, honest refusal to model run/swim load. Given this codebase's bike-only direction, refusing cleanly is *better* than half-building: partial coverage is rated worse than none by users themselves.
- **P1** — Optional manual/imported declaration of external run/swim load, with a visible conversion caveat that 1 point of run CTL ≠ 1 point of bike CTL ([TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/how-to-use-tss-to-prepare-for-an-ironman/) — *unaudited*).
- **P2** — Bike-block logic defined by what it protects (session type chosen for residual run cost). Only if the segment proves commercially material — and note that the dominant observed behaviour is already "cycling platform for the bike, coach or Garmin for the rest," which a bike-only product serves by design.

### 7.7 Women cyclists, including perimenopausal
- **P0** — Cycle + heat as post-workout attribution reasons (now table stakes), with hormonal contraceptive users handled as a first-class case rather than an error state.
- **P0** — Zero-effort optional logging: onset only, one tap. Do not require symptom-by-symptom entry — none of the surveyed competitive cyclists do it today.
- **P1** — Education and symptom management as the delivered value, not a periodised template. "Most participants said they lacked knowledge about the MC and how to manage MC related symptoms" ([PLOS ONE, 2026-06](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0343892) — *supported*), and 73% have male coaches with the topic treated as taboo.
- **P1** — Perimenopause as the sharper surface: in 187 female endurance athletes aged 40–60, sleep problems (88%) and physical/mental exhaustion (83%) were near-universal, and the paper cites a Strava survey in which only 11% had received any exercise-during-menopause advice ([PLoS One, 2025-12](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12711079/) — *supported, with the auditor's note that the 11% figure is this paper citing another study and should be attributed to the underlying source*).
- **P2** — Individual pattern discovery, offered as a question and never imposed: "across your last six cycles your hard sessions in days 24–28 came in under target — want me to stop scheduling VO2 there?" This is the only version the evidence supports.
- **Never** — a four-phase women's plan, or any claim of performance causality from phase.

## 8. Where the evidence is contested or thin

Twelve genuine conflicts, none resolved by this corpus. This section is longer than is comfortable, which is the point.

**8.1 Readiness gating is both mocked and loved — and the anti-case is weaker than it first reads.** Against: "If I listened to garmin I'd have had rest daggers 5x/week"; "The algoritms are honestly trash" ([TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/garmin-low-training-readiness-move-workout-or-skip/79182)). For: "I found RL/GL very accurate, scarily so"; "But you think after a 3 hour group ride you're fresh as a daisy?…its not wrong" ([TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/95847)). **Critically, the Garmin thread refutes its own framing** — the OP ignored his low readiness, trained anyway, and posted that he ended up with a fever; two other posters find low readiness a useful early warning. And the neat resolution the source data proposed — "the complaint is about opacity and staleness, not the concept" — is **not on the page it was cited to**. Unresolved: whether opacity or inaccuracy is the real objection.

**8.2 HRV-guided prescription: a meta-analysis and an RCT point opposite ways, in our exact population.** Manresa-Rocamora et al. (2021): small, non-significant effects on VO2max (SMD 0.13), maximal aerobic capacity (0.20), endurance performance (0.20); the only reliable difference was standing RMSSD/SD1 (SMD 0.50, 95% CI 0.09–0.91) and "fewer negative responders" ([IJERPH](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8507742/) — *unaudited*). Javaloyes et al. (2019), 17 well-trained road cyclists, 8 weeks, randomised: peak power +5.1%, power at VT2 +13.9%, 40-min TT +7.3%, with no significant gains in the traditional-periodisation arm ([IJSPP 14(1):23–32](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29809080/) — *unaudited*). n=17 against a pooled estimate is not a resolution. **Do not market HRV-guided training as performance-enhancing.** The defensible claim is "fewer bad blocks," which is also a worse sales line.

**8.3 Do 8–12 hour events require long rides?** Rider intuition says yes: "The high level guys are doing 7 to 8hr rides in prep for long gravel races"; "someone training for a 6hr even should at least be able to do 6hrs of riding in a week" ([TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/training-plan-only-has-longest-ride-of-2-hrs/93263), [TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/tr-says-4-hours-a-week-training-for-a-164k-2-100m-elevation-gran-fondo/98966) — *the latter unaudited*). A professional gravel racer, replying in the same thread, says no: "you don't have to do extremely long training rides to prepare for a 245k gravel race," recommending structured work instead. Both credibility failures are real — a 2-hour longest ride for a 245 km event destroys trust, and so does 4 h/week for a 6-hour goal event. The product cannot resolve the physiology; it can be transparent that the question is contested.

**8.4 The masters "consensus" is coaching lore the data does not support.** For: forum convergence on two hard days and 3:1 cycles; one coaching organisation specifying 48–72 h spacing ([Uphill Athlete](https://uphillathlete.com/aerobic-training/the-5-2-framework-how-masters-athletes-should-structure-their-week/) — uncited framework, mountain sport, not cycling). Against: masters riders arguing "for many of us surely we should be doing more intensity as we age, not less?" ([TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/masters-plan-not-way-to-go/99164)); weekly TSS spanning 300–1000 across ages 50–70 **with the gradient inverted** — a 70-year-old at 400 and a 56-year-old at 1000 ([TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/tss-for-a-50-yr-old/101018) — *supported; the true spread is ~3.3×, not the 2.5× originally claimed, which strengthens rather than weakens the point*); a controlled HIIT study finding "metabolic, cardiovascular, and subjective recovery was similar between the younger and older athletes" (24.5 ± 3.7 vs 47.3 ± 8.6 y) ([*Sports* 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822894/) — *unaudited*); and a 2024 review stating "The effect of age on RE has not reached a consensus in humans" ([*Cells*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10854791/) — *unaudited*). **A related claim — that the slower-recovery evidence comes mainly from running/eccentric protocols and should not be imported into a cycling app — is** **overstated**: the cited page contains no such comparison and no Doering protein data, and the analyst's own source note concedes the underlying journal returned a 403. The conclusion may still be right; the citation does not support it. Finally, the 2025 systematic review of injury and illness across all competitive cycling disciplines contains **no age stratification at all** ([PMC, 2025-07](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12278157/) — *unaudited*) — there is no masters-specific cycling injury base rate to design against.

**8.5 Women's cycle: perception and measurement disagree, and both are well evidenced.** 80% of competitive female cyclists report negative performance effects in some phases and 65% modify training ([PLOS ONE, 2026-06](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0343892)). But in 108 elite athletes across 554 cycles and 16,491 daily entries, symptoms measurably degraded well-being (fitness score 6.2 vs 5.5; mood 6.7 vs 6.4, both p<0.001) while **cyclists specifically showed no meaningful power change** on symptomatic days — footballers lost high-speed running (8.9%→8.0%), cyclists did not ([Frontiers, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1693190/full) — *unaudited*). Pooled effect across 78 studies is trivial ([McNulty et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7497427/) — *unaudited*). Yet 13 elite French cyclists over 30 months showed an intensity index "8% higher during the FP…in the Intensive effort sessions" only, with no differences at lower intensities ([Frontiers, 2024](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1307436/text) — *unaudited*). **And a finding cited as evidence of consistent women's experience is n=5 recreational strength-training women aged 24–32, whose own paper emphasises "substantial individual variation" and concludes personalised tracking beats generalised phase-based protocols** ([Frontiers, 2025-02](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1519825/full) — **overstated** on both population and direction). The honest product sits on the perception side: validate the feel, adjust attribution, educate — without claiming watts.

**8.6 Adapt-constantly vs adapt-rarely has no settled optimum.** TrainerRoad is attacked for rigidity; Xert for the opposite — the advisor jumping 30 → 276 XSS within hours, flipping Tired → Fresh overnight with no rides between ([Xert](https://forum.xertonline.com/t/adaptive-training-advisor-nonsense/38241)). Meanwhile CTS says 1–2 missed sessions should trigger no change at all. So "more adaptive" is not a direction; it is a responsiveness/stability tradeoff nobody in this corpus has solved, and the corpus contains no evidence identifying the right frequency. **Our working resolution — adapt to declared future absence, ignore small past misses — is a hypothesis, not a finding.**

**8.7 Depth vs simplicity is a genuine say/do conflict with no validated answer.** The same users who demand configurability reject the UI that provides it ([Intervals.icu](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/101779)). Progressive disclosure is proposed in-thread ("Standard View"/"Power User View") but **no evidence here shows it actually works** — it is a plausible hypothesis, not a validated pattern. Note also that Intervals.icu is simultaneously the UI failure case *and* the product people cancel paid subscriptions for, which should temper any estimate of what UI density actually costs.

**8.8 AI vs human coach: forum consensus and RCT evidence conflict.** Cyclists say the bond is the product ([TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/98421)). An RCT (N=52) found working alliance statistically indistinguishable — human M=74.50 vs AI M=72.73 on 84 points, p=0.48 ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1364054/abstract) — *supported, with heavy scope limits the claim must carry*): one 60-minute session, a *simulated* AI coach, general life coaching rather than athletic coaching, and a sample the authors concede is underpowered for medium effects. Working alliance after one session is a weak proxy for a multi-season accountability bond. The relational barrier may be lower than cyclists believe; the evidence is not strong enough to bet positioning on it.

**8.9 Carbohydrate targets are contested at every level.** Pro discourse says 120 g/h; Plews argues 90 g/h on oxidation efficiency (~86% vs ~75%) and GI risk, restricting 120 to professionals "with logistical support" and noting the underlying study used only male participants ([EndureIQ](https://www.endureiq.com/blog/carbohydrate-ingestion-rates-during-exercise-90-vs-120-grams-per-hour-which-is-best) — *unaudited*); amateurs actually consume 22–85 g/h; one age-grouper found a hard cliff between 105 and 120 g/h ([prommer.net](https://prommer.net/en/training/nutrition/120g-carb-arms-race/) — *unaudited*). **The heat interaction was overstated in the source data**: GSSI's emphasis is that heat raises carbohydrate *reliance*, and while glucose-only oxidation falls to ~0.7 g/min in heat, a 2:1 glucose:fructose blend restores ~1.14 g/min ([GSSI SSE #262](https://www.gssiweb.org/en/sports-science-exchange/article/hydration-and-nutrition-considerations-forendurance-cycling-exercise-in-the-heat)). The fluid half of that finding does hold (sweat 0.5–2.0 L/h, up to 3–4 L/h warm and humid; ≥2% body-mass loss impairs performance). There is no single number to prescribe — which is exactly why the testable-ladder feature is the right shape.

**8.10 The rankings in this corpus are not evidence; the mechanisms are.** Multiple high-confidence findings were audited as overstated for the same reason: superlatives resting on a single thread or a single race report. Forum threads are structurally biased (complaint threads select for complainers; the TrainingPeaks evidence came from a competitor's forum; App Store reviews are self-selected). **Treat every claim here as evidence that a failure mode exists and matters to real people, and none of them as evidence of relative frequency. Any roadmap ranking derived from this corpus — including §7 — is a judgement, not a measurement.**

**8.11 Willingness to pay for the "what should I do today" layer is not demonstrated.** The finding cited as proof undercuts itself: ZwiftFit gives 60 free credits on signup and 300 credits for $2.99 (~8–10¢ per workout), purchasable in only four countries, with no adoption or revenue figures published ([Zwift Insider, 2025-06](https://zwiftinsider.com/zwiftfit/) — **overstated**; that is close to free). The strongest pricing signals point the other way: free Intervals.icu at 160,000+ active athletes ([intervals.icu](https://www.intervals.icu/) — **overstated** as *displacement* evidence, since the page is vendor marketing containing no such data), free MyWhoosh pulling price-sensitive riders while field depth pulls them back ([Singletrack World, 2025-09](https://singletrackworld.com/forum/bike-forum/zwift-vs-mywoosh/) — *supported*), ~$42/month combined subscriptions deterring adoption, and a 40% Zwift rise triggering immediate cancellation. **Nothing here establishes that daily personalised prescription commands a premium.**

**8.12 Is the incumbent actually vulnerable, or just grumbled about?** For: five years of stagnation, unresponsive support, free migration tooling (tp2intervals) collapsing switching costs, free alternatives named readily. Against: "It still works okay and there is no real alternative for structured training plans"; retention anchored on archives back to 2009, the 80/20 plan library, the ATP, and a better mobile app ([Intervals.icu](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/86465), [TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/switching-from-training-peaks-premium-to-intervals-icu/93524)). One thread describes migration as easy while DC Rainmaker commenters describe it as hard. **Related overstatement:** the claim that price rises trigger cancellation "only when paired with a perception that recent AI features didn't work" is **overstated** — the cited thread contains AI dissatisfaction and one switch to a cheaper competitor, but no user attributing cancellation to that combination, and a forum thread cannot establish an "only when" causal mechanism for churn under any circumstances. That needs cancellation data we do not have. **Incumbent inertia in this category is stronger than complaint volume suggests.**

### Thin areas, named as thin

- **Workout library quality** rests on a single comparative quote. Low confidence; do not build a strategy on it.
- **Shift workers** rest on two forum posts describing rotations. The need is real; the market size is unknown.
- **Brick-session dosing** for triathletes rests on one coaching blog's assertion ("Neuromuscular adaptation happens in 3-4 brick sessions total, not 16") with no study cited — flagged low confidence in the source data and reproduced here only as opinion ([Roadman Cycling](https://roadmancycling.com/blog/triathlon-cycling-training-plan)).
- **Retention benchmarks** come from one source that self-describes as an "editorial estimate, anchored to published industry ranges" ([RetentionCheck](https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/fitness-apps) — *supported, including its own caveat*). Its figures — fewer than three workouts in the first 14 days → 3–4× churn; motivation loss 38% vs cost 18% as stated cancellation reasons — are directionally useful and must not be quoted as measurement.
- **Masters strength-training scheduling** has good survey data on *barriers* but nothing on whether an app fixing the scheduling changes behaviour.
- **The entire German-speaking market.** Zero sources. This is not a thin area; it is an empty one.
